## Executive Summary

This directive represents a **critical business infrastructure investment**, not a cosmetic redesign. The website is the primary digital interface for LightSpeed's AI Company Builder proposition, governing model, and Africa-first economics narrative. My assessment identifies a **total investment range of $185K–$340K** over 12–16 weeks, with credible ROI pathways through assessment fees, engagements, and deployments. Key risks center on the two-track approach and hidden repository/architectural costs.

## 2. "Open/Local-First AI Economics" Narrative — Cost Modeling Credibility

### Assessment: **Partially Credible — Requires Quantification**

The directive (Section 4, lines 219–301) establishes a compelling **qualitative framework** but lacks **quantitative cost modeling** for external validation.

| Claim | Current State | Financial Risk |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| "Use least expensive model that reliably does the job" | Principle stated, no cost curves | Cannot defend savings claims to prospects |
| Local inference vs. API cost avoidance | Architecture described, no benchmarks | No evidence for "material quality/latency advantage" threshold |
| African cost realities | Narrative only | No TCO comparison vs. commercial API dependence |
| Model routing tiers (Low/Mid/Premium/Human) | Conceptual | No token-cost-per-task data |

### Recommendation
**Commission a formal AI Economics Model** ($15K–$25K, 3 weeks) as part of Phase 1 deliverables:
- Token-cost curves by model tier (local Llama-3.1-8B vs. GPT-4o vs. Claude-3.5-Sonnet)
- Inference infrastructure TCO (GPU hrs, electricity, bandwidth) for Malawi/SADC contexts
- Break-even analysis: when does local inference beat API?
- Publish as Pharos "AI Economics" research note (dual-purpose: marketing + credibility)

**Without this, the narrative is marketing — not a defensible business model.**

## 7. Two-Track Approach — Budget Risks

### Risk Matrix

| Risk | Probability | Impact ($) | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|------------|------------|
| **Track B scope creep delays Track A** | High | $50K–$100K (delayed revenue) | Hard gate: Track A launch criteria (Section 41) frozen; Track B in separate branch |
| **Shared resource contention (senior eng/design)** | High | $30K–$60K | Dedicate 1 senior dev + 1 designer to Track A full-time; Track B uses fractional |
| **Athena separation incomplete at launch** | Medium | $25K–$40K (rework + risk) | Athena separation = Track A gate (QA line 2001) |
| **Backend boundary leaks** | Medium | $20K–$50K (security + credibility) | Automated tests for "no internal data in public build" |
| **Content registry sync failures** | Medium | $15K–$25K | CI pipeline validates registry → site consistency |
| **Media production bottleneck** | High | $20K–$40K | Phase 5 media can use programmatic generation (k-dense-infographics, ls-diagramming) for 60% of assets |
| **Claims governance process slows publishing** | Medium | $10K–$20K | Pre-approve claim templates; batch review weekly |

### Budget Guardrails for Two-Track

1. **Track A Cap: $250K** (Phases 0–4 + launch QA) — fixed scope, fixed timeline (10 weeks)
2. **Track B Budget: $100K** (Phases 5–7 + repo hardening) — time-boxed to 6 weeks post-launch
3. **Shared Resource Pool:** Max 20% of Track A team capacity to Track B before launch
4. **Change Control:** Any Track A scope addition requires Finance sign-off + Track B offset
