# X Thread - Post 8: AI Governance in Malawi

🧵 Every conversation we have with a Malawian or SADC institution about agentic AI runs into the same four objections: won't work in low bandwidth. where does the data go. tech debt and lock-in. AI failed before. These are not objections to argue away; they are design requirements. Here is how governance answers each. 🧵

1/6 🔌 Objection 1: "Won't work in low bandwidth." Honest answer: we operate in this reality daily. Offline-capable runtimes, messaging-native interfaces, PWA queues that buffer until the link returns. And the governance angle: if your oversight requires a dashboard nobody can load, you have a screenshot habit, not oversight.

2/6 🔒 Objection 2: "Where does the data go?" Malawi's DPA 2017 (amended 2024) plus GDPR-grade handling as default. Data stays in-country. Model deployments registered like agents: scope, permissions, named owner. The registry is what an auditor reads to answer "who can see what."

3/6 💸 Objection 3: "Technology debt and lock-in." The engineered answer is inspectable economics: 90-day pilot, no rip-and-replace, no multi-year lock-in, variable cost visible from week one. The cost curve is a dial, not a contract. We track our own debt too: ADR-032 consolidated stray agents into one registry.

4/6 🛡️ Objection 4: "AI failed before." The answer is not that AI is different now. It is that governance is the product. Five-tier HITL (ADR-017): autonomous → HITL-approved → reviewed → snoozed → cleared. Approval sweep, audit log, circuit breakers, honesty badges: mechanisms, not slogans.

5/6 🏛️ The Malawi landscape is moving: DPA 2017/2024, MACRA, the National AI Strategy consultation, the SADC Agentic AI Governance Framework, UNDP pilots. Every one asks: who decides, on what basis, how would we know? That is the G layer's design question verbatim.

6/6 📥 You do not need perfect regulation to install gates. You need a registry, a queue, an approval matrix, and an audit log. Download the Malawi Agentic AI Monitor to explore how these patterns apply in-Malawi context.
