# Carousel Copy - Post 8: AI Governance in Malawi

**Card structure (per plan §4):** 4 cards - Reservations → Answers → Policy → Adoption

## Card 1: The Four Reservations

**Title:** Four Objections, Four Answers

**Key Point:** Every conversation about agentic AI in Malawi runs into the same four objections: won't work in low bandwidth. where does the data go. tech debt and lock-in. AI failed before. These are not objections to argue away; they are design requirements.

## Card 2: The Engineered Answers

**Title:** Answers, Not Reassurances

**Key Point:**
- Bandwidth → offline-capable runtimes, messaging-native queues, PWA buffering
- Data → DPA 2017/2024 + GDPR defaults, in-country by default, registry an auditor can read
- Debt → 90-day pilot, no rip-and-replace, visible variable cost
- Skepticism → five-tier HITL (ADR-017) with audit logs, circuit breakers, honesty badges

## Card 3: The Policy Landscape

**Title:** Policy Is Moving

**Key Point:** DPA 2017/2024. MACRA. The National AI Strategy consultation. The SADC Agentic AI Governance Framework. UNDP-funded pilots. Every one asks: who decides, on what basis, how would we know? That is the governance layer's design question.

## Card 4: What to Install First

**Title:** Install the Gates First

**Key Point:** You do not need perfect regulation to start. You need: a registry of who can do what, one queue with leases and a dead-letter path, a five-tier approval matrix, and an audit log. Governance in the architecture, not the prompt.
