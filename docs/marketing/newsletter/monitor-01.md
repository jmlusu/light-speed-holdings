## 2. Policy & Governance: Malawi's AI Bill, MACRA, and SADC's Regional Framework

### Malawi: no AI statute yet — but the foundation is being laid fast

As of mid-2026, Malawi has **no dedicated AI statute and no AI regulator**. AI is governed indirectly, chiefly through the **Data Protection Act 2024 (Act No. 3 of 2024)**, in force since 3 June 2024, which designates the **Malawi Communications Regulatory Authority (MACRA)** as the Data Protection Authority. Two provisions matter most for agentic systems: a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects, and a duty to conduct data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing — which the Act describes in terms that read like a description of AI (automated processing, profiling, large-scale monitoring) (`https://www.levellers.ai/what-is/ai-regulation-malawi`, accessed 2026-09-16).

Two policy tracks are converging. First, a **draft National AI Strategy** and a **draft National Digital Transformation Strategy** were under validation in early 2026 but not yet adopted, supported by the Ministry of Information and Digitalisation with UNDP (same source; UNESCO RAM pilot status below). Second, in August 2026 the government announced it is **drafting an AI Bill** to establish a regulatory framework for public-sector AI; ICT Minister Shadric Namalomba indicated the bill should be tabled before December 2026 (`https://itweb.africa/article/malawi-drafts-ai-bill-to-govern-public-sector-tech/PmxVE7KEOnpqQY85`, 2026-08-06; `https://www.telecompaper.com/news/malawi-drafts-ai-bill--1579445`, 2026-08-10).

Malawi is also closing the readiness-data gap. On 22 July 2026 the National Commission for Science and Technology (NCST) and UNESCO validated the **Malawi AI Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM) report**, assessing legal/regulatory, social/cultural, scientific/educational, economic, and technical/infrastructure readiness. Malawi is among the six Southern African countries (of 70+ globally) piloting UNESCO's RAM (`https://ncst.mw/ncst-takes-part-in-validation-workshop-for-the-malawi-artificial-intelligence-readiness-assessment-methodology-ram-report/`, 2026-07-29). MACRA and UNDP, meanwhile, are exploring collaboration on AI, digital identity, and data protection (`https://itweb.africa/article/malawi-explores-ai-digital-ids-collaboration/nWJadMbNlybMbjO1`, 2026-07-10).

### SADC: a regional strategy and regulatory framework in motion

The SADC Secretariat is developing a **Regional AI Strategy and Regulatory Framework** — draft terms of reference are already in circulation (`https://itweb.africa/article/southern-africa-advances-ai-governance/Gb3Bw7Wadmkq2k6V`, 2026-06-26; `https://newziana.co.zw/sadc-pushes-ai-powered-climate-resilient-infrastructure/`, 2026-06-25). In August 2026, SADC member states convened in Harare for a **Regional Workshop on Data Governance in the Digital Age** (with the World Bank, Smart Africa, POTRAZ, and UNESCO), where the recurring warning was that weak data governance leaves the region a passive consumer of externally built AI (`https://itweb.africa/article/sadc-puts-spotlight-on-ai-data-governance/JN1gP7OAw5wqjL6m`, 2026-08-13; `https://news.broadcastmediaafrica.com/2026/08/14/sadc-nations-move-to-harmonise-data-governance-for-ai-readiness/`, 2026-08-14).

On our side, the Pharos policy track has prepared a **draft SADC Agentic AI Governance Framework** aimed at regulators such as MACRA and CRASA — proposing graduated autonomy tiers (advisory, automated workflow, high-risk execution with mandatory human checkpoints, and critical infrastructure where autonomous execution is prohibited), machine-readable agent credentials, immutable audit trails, and data-sovereignty standards (`docs/Pharos/policy-drafts/sadc-agentic-ai-governance-framework.md`, internal draft).

**Read:** the region is moving from "no rules" to "rules being written" in a single quarter. The window to shape those rules — with engineering evidence, not just position papers — is now.

## 3. From the Build Log: Pharos Content Intelligence

The technical note this month is the **Pharos Content Intelligence P1/P2 build, shipped 2026-09-14** (`docs/STATUS.md`):

- **Deep-research routines.** The routine engine now supports `standard` and `deep` research depth, with two deep routines — a Saturday deep-research brief and a Thursday SADC research scan — running under a defined budget, driven by prompt templates under `templates/pharos/routines/`.
- **Publishing rails with dry-run receipts.** A FileStore-backed `PublishQueue` (`src/ai_company/publishing/queue.py`) stages content with LinkedIn and Substack formatters (with character and word-count guards) and env-gated publishers that default to a **dry-run receipt** — so nothing goes out until an operator approves. This is governance as a feature: publishing is a controlled action, not a script.
- **Local-first transcription.** Whisper-based transcription (`src/ai_company/media/transcription.py`) runs locally for voice capture in low-bandwidth settings, with a graceful `unavailable` result when the model is not installed.
- **A stdio MCP server.** The repo now ships a JSON-RPC 2.0 MCP server exposing knowledge-graph query, memory recall, research lookup, Pharos reference, and publish-queue tools, gated by API-key RBAC with content filtering and PII masking, and every call written to the audit log (`src/ai_company/mcp/`).

The pattern worth stealing: the same discipline we apply to agent actions — permissions, audit, dry-run before execute — now applies to our own publishing. The Monitor you are reading is produced on that pipeline.

## 4. How We Think: Humans Stay in the Loop

Our framework is **H → A → O → M → T → G → V**: Human Purpose, Agentic Workforce, Orchestration, Enterprise Memory, Tools, Governance, Value (`docs/Pharos/positioning.md`). The ordering is deliberate — **H comes first**. The first question is never "what can agents do?" but "what should humans remain accountable for?" Our answer: strategy, ethics, leadership, relationships, and final accountability.

That answer is engineered in, not promised: 5-tier human-in-the-loop approvals and immutable audit trails are part of the company architecture, and they are the honest response to the region's skepticism — the reservation that "we tried AI before; it failed." Governance-first deployment is how we earn the right to deploy at all (`docs/Pharos/positioning.md`, "The Four Reservations — Trust-by-Engineering").

Our draft SADC framework applies the same logic at regional scale: tiered autonomy where the highest-risk actions (power grid, telecom switching, water) are decision-support only, and high-risk financial or health actions require mandatory human authorization checkpoints (`docs/Pharos/policy-drafts/sadc-agentic-ai-governance-framework.md`).

**The insight:** human-in-the-loop is not friction to design around. It is the trust mechanism that makes agentic AI deployable in regulated, resource-constrained environments — which is precisely where Malawi and SADC sit.
